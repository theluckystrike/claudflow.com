How the math works

Attempts multiply cost by the failure rate. Escalations add a premium on the escalated share. Rework loops the whole pipeline. Human review minutes convert to dollars at your hourly rate, and they scale with rework because you review the reworked tasks too.

Frequently asked

Why is cost per task higher than cost per token?

Cost per token counts only what you billed for, once. Cost per task counts the failed attempts that retried, the share escalated to a stronger model, the rework loop that repeats the whole cycle, and the human review minutes priced at their real rate. In our worked example those roll a $0.80 attempt into $8.73 per finished task.

What success rate makes agents worth it?

Compare model cost per deliverable against human minutes replaced. If the agent's model cost is $1.53 per finished task, it wins whenever a human would spend more than about 1.5 minutes doing the same work at $60/h. If quality slips and a human must fix every output anyway, the agent never wins - count the fix in the rework rate or the human minutes.

Does escalation to a stronger model pay for itself?

Only when it lowers the failure or rework rates. A 2.5x escalation premium costs real money per task; it pays when the better model cuts retries and rework more than the premium costs. Track both rates before and after.

Should human review minutes be in the model?

Yes. Review time scales with rework (you review reworked tasks too) and it often dominates the number. A calculator that hides human minutes is reporting your token bill, not your cost.
